DECISION RECORD — FIRST RELEASE SCOPE: CHEMISTRY AND CLINICAL ONTOLOGIES
Status: binding scope decision before closing Pull 07 / VAL-01.
Target release: DLCRO v0.1.
Question: should a complete chemical ontology of dental resins/monomers and a complete clinical ontology of dental procedures be incorporated into the core of the first release?

1. DECISION
Do not incorporate complete chemical or clinical ontologies into the DLCRO v0.1 core.
In the first release, incorporate selective interoperability through external mappings limited to the terms actually used in the use cases, CQs, and demonstrative graph.
Chemistry: ChEBI will be the priority reference for identifiable chemical entities; CHEMINF may be used only when chemical descriptors/representations are required. There will be no full import.
Clinical: the Oral Health and Disease Ontology (OHD) will be the first open target for dental procedures/context. SNOMED CT may be used as an optional/documentary crosswalk, but it will not be a bundled dependency of the open release.

2. SCIENTIFIC RATIONALE — PROJECT EVIDENCE BASE
Venhoven, de Gee, and Davidson studied BisGMA–TEGDMA resins and showed that camphorquinone and reducing-agent concentration affects curing/conversion rate. Chemistry is relevant, but it rapidly expands the scope toward composition, concentration, and kinetics, far beyond DLCRO’s central question.
Price, Felix, and Andreou (Biomaterials, 2005) demonstrated interaction among light source, composite, depth, and hardness response. This requires material context, but not a complete chemical ontology in the core.
Santini et al. (Journal of Dentistry, 2012) and related works in the project evidence base show photoinitiator/spectrum dependence for degree of conversion and microhardness, supporting selective mappings of photoinitiators and monomers.
Krishnaswamy and Sunitha (2007) show real clinical use of LCUs in an orthodontic procedure, but DLCRO’s scientific problem remains the light-curing/radiometry/metrology chain rather than complete representation of the dental record.

3. EXTERNAL LANDSCAPE — CHEMISTRY
ChEBI is an open, manually curated chemical entity database/ontology and publishes new ontology files monthly, including FULL, CORE, and LITE variants.
Camphorquinone already has an identifiable ChEBI entity as bornane-2,3-dione (CHEBI:34607), demonstrating that at least an important part of the domain can be reused without local duplication.
CHEMINF 2.1.0 is useful for descriptors and cheminformatics information, not as a replacement for ChEBI for the identity of all substances.
Conclusion: duplicating ChEBI/CHEMINF in the core would work against reuse and would create unnecessary maintenance.

4. EXTERNAL LANDSCAPE — CLINICAL
OHD — Oral Health and Disease Ontology — is in production, has release 2026-03-16, and is licensed CC-BY-4.0. It was created for diagnosis and treatment of dental conditions and for representing dental practice records.
OHD already contains hierarchies of dental procedures and materials; therefore, it is a better interoperability target than creating a second clinical ontology within DLCRO.
SNOMED CT has a General Dentistry package updated in 2026, but its use/distribution is governed by affiliate/national licensing. It therefore should not be a required dependency of an open ontology in the first release.
OBI remains a useful candidate for assays/investigations, but it is not necessary to import it in full to answer the current CQs.

5. WHY THE COMPLETE MODEL DOES NOT ENTER v0.1
Scope explosion: dental resins involve monomers, oligomers, photoinitiators, coinitiators, fillers, silanes, pigments, inhibitors, concentrations, chemical structure, reactions, and polymerization products.
Clinical explosion: complete dental procedures would require anatomy, diagnosis, patient, tooth/surface, condition, intervention, materials, medications, professional role, temporality, and the clinical record.
Validation cost: each new strong class/relation expands the OWL profile, reasoner, CQ/SPARQL, and SHACL requirements.
Governance cost: external resources evolve at different rates; full import would compromise core stability.
Scientific dilution: DLCRO’s novelty is the integration of light-curing, radiometry, and metrological evidence, not replacement of ChEBI, OHD, or SNOMED CT.

6. WHAT ENTERS THE FIRST RELEASE
CHEM-01 — Selective Chemical Alignment: map only chemical entities required by the demo/CQs, starting with photoinitiators and monomers explicitly used; record gaps without creating fragile equivalences.
CLIN-01 — Selective Clinical Alignment: map only the dental context/procedure required by the demo/CQs to OHD; keep SNOMED CT as an optional/documented bridge.
These items belong to the DLCRO-Alignment module and do not generate external owl:imports.
Both must be completed before the Release Candidate, but they do not block VAL-01 for the already-frozen skeleton.

7. CRITERION FOR FUTURE EXPANSION
A deeper chemical/material ontology will be internalized only if new CQs require formulation, concentration, or chemical transformation not covered by external mappings.
A deeper clinical ontology will be internalized only if use cases demonstrate a need for DLCRO-specific clinical reasoning that OHD/other resources do not cover adequately.
Expansion will occur in optional modules, preserving the DLCRO core.
